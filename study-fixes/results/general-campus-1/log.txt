--- by=one_call status=answered sec=6.58 err=None read: needs=[] limit=opinion topic=how_to asked=own_words_then_campus_side q='What does Ramapo recommend for student laptops?' general='What laptop should I get for school, a Mac or Windows?'

**General answer** (from general knowledge, not Ramapo's pages): Neither is universally better. Choose a Mac if you value battery life, a polished build, and already use Apple devices; choose Windows for more price options, gaming, or software that specifically requires Windows. Check your course’s required apps and budget first, then compare models with at least 16 GB of RAM and enough storage for your work.

Ramapo says business students should get a Windows computer because many applications are only available for Windows. Students in the Susan and Nick Vallario School of Arts, Humanities, and Education may want to consider a MacBook, as many applications for that program are Mac-based. For other students, either Windows or Mac is sufficient. [1]

For sciences, math, computer science, animation, video editing, and other computer-intensive fields, consider a laptop that meets or exceeds the “High-end” recommendations. Ramapo does not recommend Chromebooks because they may not support the same applications as Windows and Mac laptops. [1]

For a Mac, the recommended model is a 13” MacBook Air or 14″ MacBook Pro, with MacOS 15 Sequoia, MacOS 26 Tahoe, or MacOS 27 Golden Gate; M3/M4 or higher CPU; 16 GB or more RAM; and 512GB SSD. High-end: 14” MacBook Pro, M5/M5 Pro or higher, 16 GB or more RAM, and 1TB SSD. [2]

See Ramapo’s [student laptop recommendations](https://www.ramapo.edu/its/student-laptop-recommendations/) for Windows specifications and suggested laptop manufacturers. [1]

=== N03 | how do i get better at public speaking
--- by=general status=answered sec=3.84 err=None read: needs=[] limit=off_topic topic=how_to asked=own_words q='' general='How do I get better at public speaking?'
--- tools=['embedding', 'read_message', 'read_message', 'read_message', 'write_general'] path=None
--- lookups=[]
**General answer** (from general knowledge, not Ramapo's pages): Practice out loud in short, regular sessions, and record yourself to spot one thing to improve at a time. Organize each talk around a clear main point with a simple opening and ending. Slow down, pause between ideas, and look at a few friendly faces rather than trying to memorize every word. Start with low-pressure audiences and build up gradually.

**General answer** (from general knowledge, not Ramapo's pages): For graphic design classes, look for a laptop with at least 16 GB of RAM, a modern Core i5/Ryzen 5 or better processor, and a color-accurate screen (ideally close to full sRGB coverage). Check your course software’s requirements before buying.

Ramapo says students in certain majors, including animation, video editing and other computer-intensive fields, should consider a computer that meets or exceeds the “High-end” column. The listed high-end specifications are Windows 11; Intel Core Ultra X7/X9 or AMD Ryzen AI 9 (HX-class); 32 GB or more RAM; 1TB SSD; and 1920 x 1080 or better screen resolution. For video/animation students, discrete graphics with at least 2GB GPU, 4GB GPU recommended. [3]
