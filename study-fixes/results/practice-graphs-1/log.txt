**General answer** (from general knowledge, not Ramapo's pages): It depends on the field and your goals. A master’s right away can make sense if it’s required for your career, you’re sure about your specialization, or funding is strong. Working first can help you test what you enjoy, build experience, and choose a program with clearer purpose—some employers may also help pay for it. Compare likely career benefits with tuition, lost earnings, and the value of gaining experience.

Ramapo describes both paths, but does not say one is best for everyone. Its 4+1 option says a master’s can mean greater opportunities for higher salaries and higher-level positions, take one year after undergraduate rather than two, save about $11,000 or 30% of the total master’s degree cost, and still allow students to enter the workforce right after undergraduate graduation. Graduate courses are in the evening, ideal for working professionals. [7]

For the work-first path, Ramapo says job experience matters: “majors don’t get you a job. Job experience does.” It recommends co-op or internship experience before graduating. [21] Its career guidance also suggests sharing your job-search strategy with your Career Advisor, while senior-year students are advised to finalize and send graduate school applications by deadlines. [33]

To help decide, speak with your advisor as soon as possible to create a personal calendar if considering graduate school. [30] Ramapo also encourages meeting with a professor to discuss graduate school; Professor Barnes would be happy to discuss PhD programs with interested students: toddbarnes@ramapo.edu. [3] The Cahill Career Development Center is another place to seek help; its decision-making guidance recommends its computer tests to help recognize your abilities. [21]
